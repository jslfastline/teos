How it works

Four steps, one clear chain

Identify

Every business gets a Digital Business Identity, linked to a NIDA-verified owner and, where available, a TIN.

Capture

Sector-fit tools record the natural unit of work — a sale, a service, an order or a trip. USSD and offline hubs included.

Inside the proposal

How TEOS works

Seven layers connect a single transaction at street level to a verified national record. Five design principles keep the system fair, and four gated phases manage the rollout.

Architecture

Seven connected layers

Each layer has one job. Together they form a pipeline from a transaction to a verified entry in the national system.

01

National Business Identity

A single DBI for every economic actor, including informal operators, linked to NIDA, TIN and location.

02

Smart Capture

Sector-fit tools: POS scans, service buttons, kitchen orders, trip records and USSD. The receipt is an output, not extra work.

03

Smart Business Hub

An offline-first local hub that stores transactions and syncs when connectivity returns, so no sale is lost.

04

Customer Participation

QR-verified receipts with reward points, lottery eligibility or cashback turn customers into informal verifiers.

05

Risk Intelligence

AI-assisted scoring across evidence streams. It prioritises cases for human review, not automatic penalties.

06

National Economic Dashboard

Real-time, aggregated visibility of revenue and economic activity for TRA and policymakers, without exposing business-level detail.

07

Government Integration

Secure links with NIDA, BRELA, BOT, banks, mobile money, LGA/RITA and ZRA, governed by data-sharing agreements.

Design philosophy

Five principles

1

Every activity leaves a digital footprint

Record the sector's natural unit of work, not a separate receipt task.

2

Multiple independent sources of truth

No single actor's report is treated as final.

3

The customer as an informal verifier

A QR receipt and reward give the customer a reason to confirm.

4

AI-assisted risk scoring, not universal inspection

Direct scarce audit capacity to the few well-documented cases.

5

Assessment without manual negotiation

Liability is computed from evidence; the officer reviews rather than negotiates.

Safeguards

Human oversight is built in

A risk score is a prioritisation signal, not a verdict. The proposal deliberately keeps a human decision and an appeal route between the algorithm and any penalty.

Review before penalty

The business is notified of the specific discrepancy and given a defined period to respond with evidence. An officer records a reasoned decision.

Right of appeal

Both the AI's reasoning and the officer's decision are visible, logged and reviewable. The business can challenge an assessment.

Data protection

Lawful purpose, minimisation, access controls, encryption, retention rules and consent for optional reward data under the Personal Data Protection Act, 2022.

Inclusion

Connectivity cannot be a precondition

Smart Business Hub

Stores transactions locally and syncs when the network returns. A temporary outage does not lose a sale.

USSD for basic phones

Record a sale, check today's total or retrieve a receipt number without mobile data or a smartphone.

Sector-fit capture

A barber logs a haircut with one tap; a restaurant logs an order; a shop scans a barcode. The tool follows the work.

Roadmap

A 24-month, gated rollout

Each phase ends with a formal go/no-go review against agreed metrics, rather than proceeding on a fixed calendar alone.

Phase 1

Pilot

Months 1–6

One region and a limited sector set: retail, motorcycle taxis and one informal sector. Core platform, DBI issuance, data-sharing agreements and baseline risk model.

Phase 2

Medium businesses

Months 7–12

Two or three more regions. Smart Business Hub deployed, customer reward layer launched, officer training completed.

Phase 3

Large businesses

Months 13–18

Full government integration with NIDA, BRELA, banks and mobile money. National dashboard live internally within TRA.

Phase 4

Nationwide

Months 19–24

All regions and sectors, including the USSD channel. Public dashboard elements and an independent impact evaluation.

FAQ

Questions about TEOS

Ten short answers about the proposal, the DBI and the safeguards. Together they give the overall picture of what TEOS is trying to change.

01What is TEOS?

TEOS — the Tanzania Economic Operating System — is a proposed national digital layer that helps TRA see the economy through cross-verified evidence rather than taxpayer declarations alone. It connects a business identity, simple transaction capture, supplier records, bank and mobile-money flows, and customer confirmation. The aim is wider coverage, fairer assessment and better audit targeting, not simply more receipts.

02What is a Digital Business Identity?

A DBI is a unique digital identity for any economic actor, from a motorcycle-taxi rider or food vendor to a supermarket. It links a NIDA-verified owner, a phone number, a sector and a location to a QR-coded record. Every later transaction in TEOS is anchored to that identity, so the business builds a continuous transaction history.

03Is a DBI the same as a TIN?

No. A TIN is a taxpayer identification number. A DBI is broader: it can be issued to an operator who does not yet have full business registration, and it is linked to the TIN where one exists. The DBI is an entry point to visibility and formalisation, not a replacement for the TIN.

04Who can get a DBI?

Any business or self-employed person can use one: motorcycle-taxi riders, street vendors, food vendors, barbers, market traders, shops, restaurants, hotels, pharmacies, clinics, garages, farmers and larger companies. A basic DBI needs only a NIDA or TIN check, a business name, a phone number and an optional location — not full BRELA registration first.

05How does TEOS know what a business actually earns?

It compares multiple independent evidence streams: recorded sales or services, supplier deliveries, bank and mobile-money flows, and customer-confirmed QR receipts. No single source is treated as final. A mismatch — for example, declared sales that are far below supplier purchases — becomes a signal for review, not an automatic penalty.

06Is TEOS just another e-invoicing system?

No. Electronic invoicing is one input. TEOS adds a national business identity, sector-specific low-friction capture, offline-first hubs, USSD for basic phones, customer verification with rewards, AI-assisted risk scoring, a national dashboard and government integration. It is designed to reach the informal sector that receipt-only systems often miss.

07How does AI-assisted risk scoring work, and can it punish me automatically?

The AI produces a risk score that helps TRA prioritise which cases deserve human review; it does not issue penalties by itself. Before any assessment, the business is notified of the specific discrepancy and given a defined period to respond with evidence. A TRA officer reviews the case and records a reasoned decision. Any departure from the AI score is logged for audit, and the business can appeal.

08Can TEOS work offline and on basic phones?

Yes — that is a core design requirement. A low-cost Smart Business Hub can store transactions locally and sync when connectivity returns, so a network outage does not lose a sale. Basic phones can use USSD to record a sale, check today's total or retrieve a receipt number without mobile data.

09Will TEOS create new taxes or penalties?

The proposal does not introduce a new tax. Its first purpose is to widen visibility and make assessment more accurate and defensible. Implementation is phased over 24 months, with pilot regions and grace periods before enforcement. Any change to rates or thresholds remains a policy decision outside the system itself. If a business disagrees with an assessment, the human-review and appeal route applies.

10How is my data protected, and how will success be measured?

TEOS is designed around Tanzania's Personal Data Protection Act, 2022: lawful purpose, data minimisation, access controls, audit logs, encryption, retention rules and consent for optional customer reward data. Government integrations require formal data-sharing agreements and independent security audits before each rollout phase. Success is reviewed at the end of every phase against metrics such as DBI registration, receipt verification, audit precision, revenue impact, adoption and data-protection compliance.
